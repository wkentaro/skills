# Strategy catalog

This is a compact, paraphrased reference to the 80 strategies in Steph Ango's [How things win](https://stephango.com/moats), published November 18, 2023 and accessed August 18, 2026. The source uses *moat* broadly; treat each entry as a prompt for finding an edge, capability, or moat, then apply the moat test in `SKILL.md`.

The catalog is descriptive. Offensive, coercive, and deceptive strategies can help diagnose threats without becoming recommendations.

## Accumulation

- **Usership** — participation makes the system more useful or harder to leave.
- **Completeness** — one place satisfies a broad set of adjacent needs.
- **Aggregation** — an environment attracts and connects many kinds of participants.
- **Diversification** — multiple resource streams reduce dependence on any one of them.
- **Omnipresence** — availability wherever demand appears turns the thing into a default.
- **Cloning** — a proven unit expands through faithful replication.

## Price

- **Affordability** — a lower cost opens demand or excludes higher-cost alternatives.
- **Luxury** — deliberate expense, workmanship, or status supports exclusivity and price.
- **Skimming** — a tiny share of many flows accumulates into meaningful value.
- **Bundling** — inclusion with something already desired changes the effective price or distribution.

## Time

- **Heritage** — a long history accumulates recognition, legitimacy, or ritual.
- **Craftsmanship** — additional time and attention produce difficult-to-match quality.
- **Organic** — maturation depends on elapsed time that cannot simply be purchased or compressed.
- **Endurance** — sustained operation outlasts alternatives that exhaust their resources.

## Uniqueness

- **Specialization** — exceptional competence in a narrow arena.
- **Versatility** — useful competence across many tasks or environments.
- **Hybrid** — a rare combination of strengths normally found apart.
- **Divergence** — meaningful departure from established norms creates distinction.
- **Authenticity** — verifiable origin matters where imitation or counterfeiting is common.
- **Rarity** — natural limits keep supply near the edge of a distribution.
- **Scarcity** — deliberate limits constrain supply.
- **Secrecy** — protected knowledge or an entangled process makes reproduction difficult.
- **Irreverence** — rejection of convention attracts people underserved by the norm.

## Offense

- **Violence** — physical force destroys or consumes an opponent.
- **Litigation** — legal cost or restriction weakens an opponent.
- **Nettlesomeness** — persistent pressure raises the opponent's error rate.
- **Sabotage** — interference creates weaknesses inside an opponent's system.
- **Parasitism** — a host's resources are redirected without equivalent contribution.
- **Scavenging** — value is recovered from abandoned, weakened, or dying systems.
- **Espionage** — private information is acquired to remove an informational disadvantage.
- **Swarming** — many individually weak agents coordinate concentrated action.
- **Highest bidder** — deliberate overpayment secures exclusivity or denies access to others.
- **Chokepoint** — control of a required passage creates bargaining power.
- **Puppeteering** — another agent's behavior is redirected to serve the strategist.

## Defense

- **Deterrence** — credible retaliation raises the expected cost of attack.
- **Reliability** — fewer failures reduce downtime, maintenance, and uncertainty.
- **Predictability** — consistent outcomes lower the cost of choosing and planning.
- **Unpredictability** — variability or surprise makes an opponent's response less effective.
- **Decentralization** — distributed capacity and redundancy remove single points of failure.
- **Security** — resistance to theft, confiscation, or unwanted access protects resources.
- **Privacy** — limiting disclosure protects information and autonomy.
- **Durability** — robustness preserves function despite damage or stress.
- **Neutrality** — credible non-belligerence makes the thing a trusted refuge or intermediary.
- **Obscurity** — staying unnoticed avoids competition or attack.
- **Antifragility** — stress or use improves the system rather than merely leaving it intact.

## Deception

- **Camouflage** — resemblance to the environment prevents detection.
- **Mimicry** — resemblance to another kind of thing changes how observers respond.
- **Decoy** — attention or attack is redirected toward a false target.
- **Lure** — an attractive signal draws another party into a trap.
- **Infiltration** — defenses are crossed without triggering recognition or resistance.

## Timing

- **First-mover** — early action secures resources, learning, standards, or mindshare first.
- **Second-mover** — a fast follower copies what worked while avoiding some discovery cost.
- **Last-mover** — patience lets others expose failed approaches before committing resources.

## Accreditation

- **Monopoly** — governing authority grants or protects exclusive control.
- **Prestige** — recognition by a trusted authority transfers status and confidence.
- **Curation** — superior selection and grouping reduce search and evaluation cost.

## Collaboration

- **Union** — shared governance reduces conflict and internal competition.
- **Alliance** — independent parties cooperate where combined strengths help both.
- **Emergence** — local contributions produce useful collective behavior without complete central direction.
- **Centralization** — concentrated decisions enable coordination and decisive action.
- **Standardization** — common rules reduce friction and make participation interoperable.
- **Symbiosis** — mutual dependency makes each party more successful with the other present.
- **Herding** — a group creates protection or bargaining power unavailable to isolated members.
- **Distributed ownership** — community stakes align participation with the system's success.
- **Transparency** — visible processes and workings make claims easier to verify and trust.

## Speed and scale

- **Iteration** — shorter learning cycles improve the thing faster.
- **Efficiency** — equivalent outcomes require fewer resources.
- **Agility** — the thing adapts quickly when its environment changes.
- **Precision** — greater exactness produces outcomes alternatives cannot reliably match.
- **Blitzing** — concentrated resources create a sudden temporary advantage.
- **Composability** — parts can be assembled into new configurations through stable interfaces.
- **Modularity** — independent units can change or combine without rebuilding the whole.

## Ease

- **Intuitiveness** — people can understand and use the thing with little instruction.
- **Fun** — enjoyment makes participation intrinsically rewarding.
- **Simplicity** — fewer interacting parts reduce failure and learning costs.
- **Low-friction** — fewer steps, delays, and incidental costs make action easier.
- **Charm** — personality or aesthetics create warmth, attachment, and preference.

## Transformation

- **Malleability** — form or behavior can be customized without losing the underlying thing.
- **Metamorphosis** — distinct forms optimize for different phases or environments.
- **Copycat** — rapid reproduction of another's method or style avoids original discovery cost.
